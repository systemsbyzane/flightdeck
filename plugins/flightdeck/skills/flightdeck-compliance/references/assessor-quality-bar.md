# Assessor Quality Bar

Use this guide to keep compliance outputs reviewable and honest.

## Default Posture

Act as a package author, assessor, and AO support analyst at the same time:

- prepare clear package language
- challenge weak or unsupported claims
- identify missing evidence
- explain residual risk and decision points
- preserve the difference between current state and recommended state

When these goals conflict, evidence and authorization risk win over package
polish.

## Required Distinctions

Every material claim should be classifiable as:

- supported fact
- reasonable inference
- assumption
- gap
- recommendation

If the output format cannot show those labels, put them in companion notes.

## Review-Ready Outputs

A review-ready output:

- preserves user-provided workbook structure
- cites or indexes source evidence
- avoids hidden assumptions
- separates inherited, hybrid, common, and system-specific responsibility
- marks stale or conflicting evidence
- lists skipped fields and why they were skipped
- flags missing owner decisions
- avoids claiming approval, closure, upload, or authorization without evidence

## Red Flags

Stop and ask for clarification or mark a gap when:

- the control baseline is unknown
- the authorization boundary is unclear
- the workbook columns are ambiguous
- source documents disagree on system scope
- eMASS upload requirements are not provided
- evidence appears to contain secrets or controlled material that should not be
  copied into prepared outputs
- a requested statement would claim compliance that the evidence does not
  support

## Writing Style

Use direct professional language. Avoid filler, legalistic padding, and generic
control restatement. Prefer concrete nouns: system, boundary, role, artifact,
tool, cadence, evidence, weakness, mitigation, residual risk.

## Completion Standard

Do not call a package artifact complete merely because all cells are filled.
Completeness requires the requested document or workbook plus enough internal
record to trace evidence, assumptions, gaps, and risk. Keep that internal record
outside the submission-facing file unless the governing template requires it.
The final deliverable must contain no unresolved template variables or
authoring-process labels.
